Mandai X Ranger Buddies

UX/UI B2C Edutainment User Testing 2026

“ How might we design a seamless, 'phygital' edutainment experience that integrates educational value with engaging play for children? ”

Overview

Ranger Buddies is an edutainment initiative by Mandai X, the venture-building arm of the Mandai Wildlife Group. It is designed to nurture a generation of "everyday heroes" by engaging children in wildlife conservation and environmental action through a blend of digital and physical experiences.

As a UI/UX design intern, I contributed to the B2C experience of the Ranger Buddies web app, focusing on in-app features, mission flows, and collaborative campaigns.

My Contributions

  • Spearheaded the validation of the web app's new reward system feature. Created high-fidelity prototypes, led physical User Testing sessions with Zoo visitors, and presented synthesised findings to the CEO to drive feature implementation

  • Created UI screens and graphic assets for digital mission games

  • Conducted live testing at the physical mission sites to ensure a seamless phygital experience

  • Conducted competitor research for a brand collaboration touchpoint, providing key insights that guided the Product Managers' strategic implementations.

    Case Study #1

    “Polar Melt Patrol” Mission

    I was the sole designer for the “Polar Melt Patrol” mission at PLQ Parkside, which focuses on how global warming is melting Antarctica and its consequences on penguins and the environment. I've done up mostly everything from scratch, conceptualising the narrative, creating physical and digital activities, and designing the screens for the mission to ensure the experience is fun and educational for children. The timeline for this project was roughly 1 month.

    View Mission Ideation Process👇

    Creating the Narrative

    I started by developing 5 sub-narratives for the activities revolving around the Antarctic using the ADKAR framework and COURAGE traits, balancing learning takeaways with an engaging, intuitive experience for parents and children.

    Digital & Physical Activities Ideation

    Following the narrative development, I ideated both physical and digital activities. To work within the technical constraints of our template-based Contentful CMS, I adapted mechanics from previous missions, and taking into account reskinning elements (e.g previously-used "playground equipment") to align with the new theme.

    Fitting Narrative + Activities

    Once completed, I mapped an overview of all activities and their narratives to view how the physical and digital aspects interacted, ensuring cohesiveness. I iterated on this framework multiple times to ensure a smooth and clear user experience, while balancing constraints like technical feasibility and physical installation limitations.

    Mission Gameplay

    Below is the final look of the mission. The most important aspect of the mission is that it is designed to be completed in a single visit (roughly 10 minutes), with a clear start and end point, allowing children to fully immerse themselves in the experience without feeling rushed or overwhelmed.

    Most importantly, with PLQ as our mall partner, the experience has been strategically streamlined to drive completion, encouraging participants to earn and spend their mall perks.

    Case Study #2

    Validating the Core Reward Loop

    I lead the validation and iterations of the brand-new reward system (MVP) for the RB web app. The goal was to incentivise kids for completing their missions, and access partner rewards in one key place. In only three weeks until rollout, I rapidly validated user flows, currency comprehension and navigation for two distinct user profiles: Parents and children.

    Phase 1: High-Fidelity Prototyping of Flows

    Below are the main flows/screens with the newly-added rewards elements:
    (For context: "Books" are being used for our main method of reward currency)

    ✏️ The "Mission Flow": A simulated 6-activity walkthrough designed to help users understand mission mechanics, where upon the completion of each activity, the new “Books” meter bar fills up by 100 points.

    ✏️ The "Redeem Rewards Flow": (New) Covering partner selection, profile options, and reward redemption process.

    ✏️ The "Home Page": Integrating a new “Books Menu” entry point directly onto the existing map interface homepage.

    Phase 2: Conducting On-Site User Testing at Mandai Wildlife Zoo

    We conducted rapid on-site guerilla user testing at Mandai Wildlife Zoo, capturing high-value, 15-minute sessions with visiting families. As the interviewer, I tailored the UT Guide for both parent and child demographics. Since the children were quite young (4-10 years old), I simplified the phrasing to ensure easier comprehension while avoiding leading questions for authentic responses. For example, after a progress bar is filled, I would ask: “What do you notice has changed? → Why do you think that happened?” to guide them along the process.

    Core Objectives:

    🧪 Assess the clarity of "Books Currency": Determine if users recognise it as redeemable.

    🧪 Evaluate intuitiveness and user comprehension of the "earn-to-spend" loop's mechanism.

    🧪 Determine the ease of navigation to the rewards section after completing a mission (without assistance).

    Phase 3: Refinements, additional testing

    Afterwards, I synthesised the responses. In our first testing, there were some challenges that both parents and children faced. I refined the prototype, making sure the solutions address the issues, prominently, the drop-off after completing the mission.

    Given the tight timeline, I prioritised high-impact solutions that leveraged on existing functionality, ensuring effective results with minimal implementation.

    Phase 4: Testing against our core objectives

    We returned to the zoo to test the new iterations with more visitors against our core objectives.

    🙌 There was a great improvement in the reception of the Rewards System...

    🤔 However, despite these improvements...

    both parents and children still significantly expressed confusion regarding books as a reward currency.

    💡 The Pivot: Changing the currency to COINS

    I presented these synthesised insights and data-driven slides to the CEO during our weekly huddle. Backed by this user data, I proposed a shift to "RB Coins" for the MVP. The team aligned on this choice.

    👍 Final Outcomes

    🛍️ For Partners: Increased family foot traffic to partners’ physical retail stores for reward redemption, driven by awareness from a centralised reward hub.

    👪 For the Public: Boosted mission completion rates by eliminating post-activity user drop-off. Created a continuous incentive loop that kept children motivated to complete every required activity.
