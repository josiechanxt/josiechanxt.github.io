PROJECT OVERVIEW

“ How might we design a seamless, 'phygital' edutainment experience that integrates educational value with engaging play for children? ”

Ranger Buddies is an edutainment initiative by Mandai X, the venture-building arm of the Mandai Wildlife Group. It nurtures everyday heroes by engaging young children in wildlife conservation and environmental action through connected digital and physical experiences. As a UI/UX design intern, I contributed to the B2C web app through in-app features, mission flows, and collaborative campaigns.

Phygital "Missions": Expanding learning beyond the zoo

Ranger Buddies features a hybrid of physical and digital AR missions designed to instil C.O.U.R.A.G.E values in children, preparing them to be future-ready beyond the classroom. The ecosystem includes a web app where parents can register their children and use it across various physical missions hosted in malls, the Zoo, and other partners, providing an educational, family-friendly experience in public spaces.

Creating the “Polar Melt Patrol” Mission

Turning Antarctic climate change into a playful, educational experience for young children (aged 4–10)

This mission was done for our mall partner, Paya Lebar Quarter (PLQ) Parkside. As the sole designer, I did the end-to-end design process of crafting the narrative, designing phygital activities, copywriting and crafting screens to make every step feel intuitive.

THE CHALLENGE 🏁
"How might we translate complex scientific concepts into a simple educational play experience that four-year-olds can enjoy independently?"

Scoping technicalities, crafting engaging storytelling

Ideation involved translating researched scientific facts into distinct, educational narratives across physical and digital touchpoints. From PLQ's requirement, the activities' mechanism needed to be unique compared to past missions while reusing past structures.

Scoping Technical Constraints

Refurbishment of existing physical structures + Repurposing existiing CMS digital screen templates/mechanisms.

Developing Activities' Narratives

I crafted subnarratives and learning takeaways for each activity, integrating the ADKAR framework and C.O.U.R.A.G.E traits.

Digital & Physical Activities Ideation

Activities were brainstormed, with some based on previous mechanics, while exploring new concepts for fresh opportunities.

[ CASE STUDY 02 ]

Validating the core reward loop

I led the validation and iterations of a new rewards-system MVP for the Ranger Buddies web app. With only three weeks until rollout, the work focused on quickly understanding the experience of two distinct user groups: parents and children.

Phase 1: High-fidelity prototyping of flows

Below are the main flows/screens with the newly-added rewards elements:
(For context: "Books" are being used for our main method of reward currency)

Mission Simulation

A simulated activity walkthrough to help new users understand mission mechanics, where upon the completion of each activity, the new “Books” meter bar fills up by 100 points.

Redeem Rewards

(New) Covering partner selection, profile options, and reward redemption process.

Home Page

Integrating a new “Books Menu” entry point directly onto the existing map interface homepage.

Phase 2: Conducting on-site user testing at Mandai Wildlife Zoo

We conducted rapid on-site guerilla user testing at Mandai Wildlife Zoo, capturing high-value, 15-minute sessions with visiting families. As the interviewer, I tailored the UT Guide for both parent and child demographics. Since the children were quite young (4-10 years old), I adapted a more guided approach, while still avoiding leading questions to gain authentic responses. For example, after a progress bar is filled, I would ask: “What do you notice has changed? → Why do you think that happened?” to help them along the process.

Core Objectives 💡

🧪 Access the clarity of "Books currency", determine if users recognise it as redeemable.

🧪 Evaluate intuitiveness and user comprehension of the "earn-to-spend" loop's mechanism.

🧪 Determine the ease of navigation to the rewards section after completing a mission (without assistance).

Phase 3: Refinements, additional testing

Afterwards, I synthesised the responses. In our first testing, there were some challenges that both parents and children faced. I refined the prototype, making sure the solutions address the issues.

Drop-off after completing Mission flow

Users successfully completed the mission but were not always aware of the next step to redeem rewards.

"Books" is unclear as a currency

5/5 Children failed to identify "Books" and it's role as a primary reward currency. 3/5 Parents preferred more intuitive icons such as coins.

Low Discoverability of components

Most users did not immediately notice the reward menu and meter bar as they were concentrated on the activity.

💡

Given the tight timeline, I prioritised high-impact solutions that leveraged on existing functionality, ensuring effective results with minimal implementation.

Phase 4: Testing against our core objectives

We returned to the zoo to test the new iterations with more visitors against our core objectives.

🙌 There was a great improvement in the reception of the rewards system...

✅ 100% of Parents and Children successfully identified the “earn-to-spend” reward loop.
👦

Children displayed improved independence, as they understood they were earning books through activities.

👩

Parents understood that there's still an outstanding task of redeeming the reward through the CTAs/animations.

🤔 However, despite these improvements...

both parents and children still significantly expressed confusion regarding books as a reward currency.

🤔 7/9 Parents & 100% of Children across UT1 & UT2 found the book icon unclear as a reward currency.
👦

Children displayed literal bias, mistaking book icon as an actual functional item: "to read".

👩

Parents mentioned lack of clarity, still preferring standard symbols like coins/fruits to improve recognition.

🗣️ Presentation to leadership

I presented these synthesised insights and data-driven slides to the CEO during our weekly huddle. Supported by the guerilla user testing data showing user confusion with "Books", I proposed pivoting to a coins-based system. The team aligned on this direction.

Final Outcomes 👍

🛍️ For Partners: Increased family foot traffic to partners’ physical retail stores for reward redemption, driven by awareness from a centralised reward hub.

👥 For the Public: Boosted mission completion rates by eliminating post-activity user drop-off. Created a continuous incentive loop that kept children motivated to complete every required activity.
