SproutSwap

UX/UI Design System 2026

“ How might we design a seamless and engaging plant swap digital experience for the community? ”

Overview

As a plant-lover, I started this quick passion project to identify a real-world problem and design a solution for it through an app. Handling the end-to-end UI/UX process from initial ideation to prototyping, I wanted to improve a fun activity from my own daily routine: browsing Facebook groups to swap plants. My goal was to take that existing habit and elevate it into a much better user experience.

    • The Core Challenges

    While Facebook groups foster community, the plant-swapping process is chaotic. Users are met with a disorganised, fast-moving feed where casual chat, urgent trade requests and general discussions all compete for attention in the same space.

    Finding specific plants to trade is inefficient due to three primary UX flaws:👇

    Anatomy of a Listing

    An analysis of a typical plant-swap post revealed five crucial pieces of information required by users:

    1. Offering: The item (e.g plant/food) available for swap

    2. Looking For: Preferred item requested for swap

    3. Location: Swap meetup point

    4. Contact Preferences: "PM me" or comment

    5. Swap Status: "Reserved" or "Taken"

    6. Media: Photos of the plant's condition or requested item

    Pain Point: Reduced Content Scannability

    However, the lack of a standardised post format prevents users from skimming information efficiently, forcing them to read every listing meticulously and drastically increasing cognitive load.

    The Process

    I mapped the user journey to streamline interactions while preserving the crucial familiar, rewarding routine of traditional plant-swapping. Rather than rewriting the process, SproutSwap digitises these existing touchpoints into a hassle-free flow.

    Ideation & Lo-Fi Sketches

    I began by translating these solutions into low-fidelity sketches to rapidly visualise the key features.

    I opted for:

    1. Intuitive Swipe Cards that shows at-a-glance information, allowing users to evaluate listings instantly without information overload.

    2. Tagging System which are assigned by users for easier filtering of plant types.

    Wireframing

    To gain a clearer understanding of the user flows and hierarchies, screens were quickly mocked up. It allowed me to get an overall sense of the app's structure, and identify any potential usability issues early on.

    (e.g Chat being made into its own section instead of being nested under the Profile section for better access.)

    Establishing a cosy art direction

    To match the community-driven nature of the app, I established an art direction that leans into a cosy, organic aesthetic, using warm tones inspired by nature and soft UI elements to make the digital plant-swapping experience feel inviting and grounded.

    Creating the Design System

    With a direction in place, I built a scalable design system to serve as a single source of truth, ensuring visual consistency and accelerating high-fidelity prototyping.

    Components were built using modular structures and properties (like booleans) for a more seamless developer implementation.

    The Solution: SproutSwap MVP

    Building this product from the ground up, I recognise the importance of scoping a lean MVP to create a strong foundation that solves the core challenges. Below are the primary features included in this initial release. As the product matures, I plan to introduce additional “nice-to-have” features to enhance the experience further.

    1. Swipe and Swap

    I implemented a familiar Tinder-style swipe mechanic to offer a trendy, convenient browsing experience that reduces cognitive load through a consistent, easy-to-skim layout. To provide richer visual context, I paired this with the Facebook-style collage view, to give users instant, richer visual context across multiple images.

    2. Tag-Based Filtering

    To improve low discoverability, tag filters help to categorise different types of plants, equipment and food. This allows users to effortlessly narrow down their search to find what they are looking for faster.

    (Note: Multiple categories can be selected at once too.)

    (On the backend side, the algorithm will show the most recently-uploaded listings first.)

    3. Match & Chat

    To eliminate user confusion over availability, the app uses a single chat interface that only appears after both users swipe on each other. This makes managing conversations much easier. Once an offer is accepted, the listing automatically deactivates, letting other interested users know it's no longer available.

    4. Profile

    Streamlined the listing process by utilising a standardised template for necessary information. Users can also manage their active listings and view past ones, while reviews from previous swappers appear on their profiles to build credibility.

    Future Features & Enhancements

    Currently, most of the features I’ve done focus on the plant swapping process. To build a more cohesive product, I have some enhancements in mind that I would like to implement in the future as this project continues. I’m excited to see how it develops!
