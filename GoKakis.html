GoKakis

Service Design Product Design Motion Graphics 2025

“ How might we transform the rehab waiting experience to be socially and physically engaging while keeping it simple and independent? ”

Overview

This project was done in collaboration with the NUS Department of Biomedical Engineering. We were tasked to transform a raw, engineering proof-of-concept into a mature, market-ready product integrated within a broader service ecosystem. Design complexity was managed by understanding technical constraints, while simultaneously using user research to inform our process. The final project showcases the value of human-centered design in turning complex utility into a seamless, accessible service.

    • The Scenario & Rehab Realities

    The Scenario

    Long waits, disengagement, and boredom. For many elderly individuals, this is a familiar routine at rehabilitation centres as they wait for their appointments.

    The Original Product

    Originally, as a biomedical engineering project, the creators reimagined waiting by weaving physiotherapy elements into a playful hybrid gaming station of bingo and tic-tac-toe.

    How it works:
    User cycles with pedal (leg workout) → Ball is dispensed → User picks up ball (stretches back) → Puts ball into Tic Tac Toe slot (cognitive).

    Pain Points

    However, we felt the product could be simplified and required significant staff oversight. Furthermore, there was a limited selection of games, which could leave seniors disengaged after several rehab visits.

    Identified Opportunities

    The original project's success proved the value of gamified physiotherapy. So we leveraged that, with the intention of improving the fun factor and intuitiveness of the games while minimising reliance on staff. (Reducing staff-reliance, gamifying physiotherapy)

    Revamped Product: GoKakis Interactive Projector

    GoKakis is an interactive projection system with an old-school charm that turns waiting times into opportunities for social interaction and fun, while gamifying physiotherapy. All it takes is an A4-sized projector, designed to resemble a traditional rattan basket, to transform any floor into a playground of memories inspired by Singapore's nostalgic past.

    We opted for an interactive projector because of its spatial versatility. We conducted extensive market research to understand its technical feasibility. Our biggest constraint was cost, as these systems in the market typically cost around $3,500 USD. To address this, we explored open-source, DIY methods using affordable components to inform our final product design.

    Games: Incorporating familiarity with nostalgia...

    Beyond the technology, we provide a humanising user experience. Our games are inspired by nostalgic Singaporean pastimes like Longkang Fishing and Chapteh (and more), allowing seniors to relive their past. By combining these familiar movements with physical therapy, we create a more engaging rehabilitation process and a highly social experience.

    Waiting together, socialising together...

    Highly scalable, anytime anywhere in any rehab centre.

    Overall, GoKakis is a system ready to be scaled across any rehabilitation center, creating a fun and interactive experience that seniors can look forward to during their appointments.
